Overview / Unit 4

Unit 4 · Semester 2 · April – June

Who Am I? Comprehensive Sexuality Education

Built around the question “Who am I?”, the unit pairs a comprehensive sexuality education strand with a media-production strand. The health strand covers identity, orientation and gender, anatomy, relationships, consent, contraception and STIs, grounded in the Chilean context. The media strand covers photography, storytelling and CapCut editing. Students make an individual identity video, then a group PSA about communication in relationships.

About 30 lessons · Apr 21 – Jun 10 Products: Who Am I video · Group PSA

Driving questions

  • Who am I? How do we see, know, and treat ourselves?
  • What makes us, US?
  • What message about communication in relationships should our community know and understand, and what action could someone take?

Students will be able to…

  • Sexual health (3.0): analyze how values, ideas and experiences influence human sexuality, and use that understanding to make informed decisions about caring for themselves and others.
  • Health communication (3.0): assertively communicate their needs and wants while respecting the needs and wants of others.
  • Creative communicator: use a range of formats and media to convey information effectively.
  • Identify reproductive anatomy and its functions, and the stages of sexual response.
  • Distinguish assertive, passive and aggressive communication, and say “no” clearly.
  • Distinguish enthusiastic consent from coercion. Consent is continuous and can be withdrawn.
  • Tell healthy relationship behaviors apart from warning signs of abuse.
  • Understand contraception as self-care and know how to access confidential healthcare in Chile.
  • Apply the CRAAP test to health information and think critically about sexual content online.

Approach & norms

The unit opens by establishing a safe learning community.

Norm = Empathy

All values and beliefs are welcome, but students may not say or do things that are hurtful or exclusionary. An explicit identity-based harm policy means zero tolerance for behavior that targets someone’s identity.

Questions welcome

Questions are always encouraged.

Right to Pass

No one has to share.

“I” statements

Speak from your own experience.

One speaker, many listeners

One voice at a time.

Respect differences

Diverse identities and beliefs are part of the room.

The Question Box

Students submit anonymous questions, and the teacher answers them at the start of the next lesson.

Accurate, clinical vocabulary is treated as a tool for self-understanding, safety and respect, and students learn that identity language evolves. Lessons emphasize bodily autonomy, normalize individual choice (“some do, some don’t”), and use true/false myth-busting. Contraception and STI content is grounded in Chilean law and healthcare (CESFAM, confidential care from age 14), and students are directed to trusted adults and professionals.

Lesson sequence

Health lessons and communicator (media) lessons are interleaved. Dates are shown where the source gives them.

Identity & the Who Am I video

  1. Introduction to human sexuality Apr 21

    Norms and the Question Box. A Philosophical Walk, then a discussion of heteronormativity and lived experience.

  2. Building a better understanding of a human

    Defining sexuality. A gallery walk on values, orientation, gender roles, relationships and body image.

  3. Identity Apr 23

    “What makes us, US?” The Identity Wheel and the Identity Iceberg (visible vs. hidden).

  4. Visible identity

    Students choose three shots that would represent their identity on video.

  5. Communicators #1: photography Apr 27

    The Who Am I project is introduced. Alignment, space, repetition, color and contrast.

  6. Photo & video techniques

    Rule of thirds, orientation and stabilization. Students write a ten-clip shot list.

  7. Aspects of sexual identity Apr 29

    Latin and Greek roots, and defining sexual orientation.

  8. Gender, media & stereotypes

    Influencer tropes and gender “boxes”, and their impact on transgender and gender non-conforming people.

  9. Communicators #2: story

    CapCut setup, story arc vs. “newspaper” structure, and an ad as a case study.

  10. Storyboarding & scripting

    “A story, not a list.” Narration and Level 1 CapCut editing.

Anatomy & sexual health

  1. Male anatomy May 6

    External and internal anatomy and function, with a Kahoot review.

  2. Stages of sexual response

    The brain as the primary sexual organ and the right to control one’s body. Myth-busting.

  3. Female anatomy May 8

    External and internal anatomy, with an anatomy-sheet check. Separating arousal from desire.

  4. Myths, facts & media

    True/false myth-busting. Pornography discussed through critical consumption.

  5. Communicators #3 & work days May 12

    Who Am I requirements, editing, and the export workflow. Filming and finishing the video.

  6. Anatomy quiz · “We’re all in relationships” May 15

    The quiz, then an introduction to relationships.

Relationships, consent & reproductive health

  1. Relationships & sexual health

    Feeling seen, valued and cared for. Relationship types and case studies in groups of three.

  2. Informed choice: birth control

    Barrier, hormonal and long-acting methods, emergency contraception, and Chilean law (Laws 20.584 and 20.418, the “3 causales”).

  3. Medical visits, myths & consent

    Confidentiality, reproductive coercion, “Check the Source”, and a four-question decision framework.

  4. Menstruation & conception

    The cycle, fertile windows, hormones, symptom management and tracking apps.

  5. Pregnancy & STI protection

    Fertilization, pregnancy options in Chile, STI prevention, and condom steps.

  6. STIs & reproductive health

    Categories, symptoms and transmission. “Nothing scary about STI tests!”

  7. STI group inquiry

    Team research on one of eight topics, using CRAAP. Teams present, then each student writes an individual synthesis.

  8. Communicating needs & boundaries May 25

    Four communication styles in text-message scenarios, then refusal practice.

  9. Communication skills chatbot

    AI role-play with a “coach-bot” reflection. Students submit the conversation log.

  10. Talking about sexual activity & consent May 27

    Review of the class chatbot data, then five harm-reduction decision questions.

  11. Consent & power

    Four power scenarios, definitions of consent and coercion, check-in scripts, and paired role-play.

  12. What makes a healthy relationship?

    The healthy-to-unhealthy spectrum and the cycle of abuse. Individual written reflection.

PSA project

  1. PSA days & viewing party due Jun 10

    Timed writing, brainstorm and voting, empathy interviews, storyboard, crew roles, filming and editing. Ends in a viewing party and awards.
