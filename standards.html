Standards & Proficiency Scales

What students are assessed on

First Year Seminar earns students both a health credit and a technology credit. Assessment is standards-based. Each standard has a Nido proficiency scale, and every graded task names the scale and the level it gives evidence for, for example “L2 – Knowledge Constructor”.

Reading a Nido proficiency scale

Every scale uses the same structure. Task labels in Schoology (L2, L3, L4) tell students which band of the scale the task gives evidence for.

2.0 Foundations

The vocabulary and simpler processes students need before they can reach the target. Typical verbs: list, explain, identify, describe, compare.

3.0 The target

The learning goal itself, the level we expect students to reach. Typical verbs: apply, analyze, interpret, synthesize.

4.0 Beyond

In-depth inference and application that show complexity, integration or transfer. Each scale gives a sample 4.0 task, but other demonstrations count too.

Half-point scores (1.5, 2.5, 3.5) mean the student has the lower band fully and part of the next one.

Open Scale in Google Docs

3.0Target
The learner will:
  • Demonstrate functional health and information literacy by exploring real-world health issues.
  • Use effective research strategies to locate reliable information, considering accuracy, validity, bias, origin, and relevance.

  • Curate and organize information with digital tools into meaningful artifacts (portfolio, presentation, infographic).
2.0Foundations
  • synthesize
  • curate
  • research strategy
  • CRAAP
  • peer review
  • bias
  • primary / secondary source
  • artifact
  • Identify valid and reliable health or digital information sources (e.g. CDC, NIH, Ministerio de Salud).
  • Evaluate the reliability of sources using frameworks like CRAAP (currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose).
  • Identify examples of bias, relevance, and accuracy in sources.
  • Use digital tools to gather and organize information with guidance.
