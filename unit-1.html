Overview / Unit 1

Unit 1 · Semester 1 · August – September

Habits of Action

Students open the year by studying their own routines. They track habits, build digital organization systems, analyze habit loops, and write SMART goals. Then they use Gemini to prototype a personal “habit helper” app, before teaming up to design tools for classmate clients at the AI Tools Expo.

17 class days Product: AI Tools Expo (individual + team app)

Guiding questions

  • How do we design our own solutions, tools and systems to help us maximize our healthy outcomes?
  • How do we learn about ourselves? What works for me?
  • What makes something a goal instead of just a dream?
  • How do we know this tool is working?
  • How do we go from ME to WE?

Students will be able to…

  • Implement new strategies to make progress toward personal, academic and health goals: tracking progress, setting reminders, taking small steps, overcoming barriers, and revising the goal as life changes.
  • Use tracked data to assess their habits and revise their plans.
  • Analyze a habit as a loop (cue → routine → reward) and plan a replacement routine that keeps the reward.
  • Write SMART goals connected to a concrete action plan: what, where, who, when, how.
  • Use a deliberate design process to build, test and revise a prototype tool, first for themselves and then for a client.
  • Build everyday digital skills: Drive and sharing, Chrome bookmarks, Google Calendar, Gmail filters, and professional email.

Approach & key routines

The unit moves from me to we: students design for themselves first, then for someone else.

FYS notebook / design journal

“Your history, your process, your map forward.” Problem frame: [Person] needs a way to ___ because ___, then five radical solutions, feedback and a sketch.

Habit loop

Cue → routine → reward. Cues and cravings don’t disappear, so students replace the routine and keep the reward.

Habit tracking sheet

Students track two habits (bedtime and homework time, plus a personal choice), choose their own reminders, and review the data weekly.

SPARK prompting

Situation · Problem · Aspiration · Results · Kick. A structured Gemini prompt that produces a single-page HTML app.

Design ownership

“Our words, our ideas, our design. AI builds it.” Students design; AI does not design for them.

Feedback protocol

Explain (1 min), the partner uses the tool (2 min), then questions (3 min), then switch. Stems include “I wonder if…” and “One thing that confused me was…”

Lesson sequence

Class days as numbered in the Unit 1 slides.

Know yourself · track your habits

  1. Welcome: “What works for me?”

    Introductions, course overview, intro survey, hand-outline reflection, notebook setup, and a habits video.

  2. Project launch & Digital Organization Level 1

    The AI Tools Expo is introduced. Students map their weekday time, set up Drive, sharing and bookmarks, and start the tracker.

  3. Goals & the science of habits

    The habit loop, how to change a habit, worked examples, and “What is a system?”

  4. Calendar & habit-loop planning

    Google Calendar skills and the teen brain. Students plan a new habit loop with a partner.

  5. Email & SMART goals

    Inbox filters and professional email structure, two scenario emails with attachments, then SMART goals and action plans.

  6. Data & the L2 Habit Reflection

    Analyze sample screen-time data, share a success and a failure, and write SMART goals for scenarios.

Design a tool to hack your life

  1. Introduction to Gemini

    Why Nido uses Gemini. Build a flashcard app in Canvas from a modeled prompt, iterate, and fix errors.

  2. SPARK & the Habit Helper

    Build the SPARK prompt step by step from each student’s habit loop and SMART goal, then use the app at home.

  3. Design journal & Prototype v1

    Step away from AI: needs, a problem statement, five radical ideas, and a wireframe. Then build and screenshot Version 1.

  4. Reflect, export, plan v2

    Reflect against the habit loop, export the code to .html, plan Version 2, and peer test.

  5. Tool design & screencast

    Reflect on weekend use, keep iterating, then plan and record a screencast.

  6. L4 written reflection

    A 30-minute reflection on the goal, whether the tool worked, and personal systems.

From ME to WE · the AI Tools Expo

  1. From ME to WE

    A collaboration Y-chart, team formation, and two rounds of client interviews.

  2. Defining & designing

    A client problem statement, five fantastical ideas each, client feedback, and role assignments (Spokesperson, Technical Designer, Marketing Manager).

  3. Designing & refining

    Role deliverables. The team SPARK prompt is built from interviews, with every version saved.

  4. Presentation practice

    Finish the app, SPARK document, slides and script, then complete a collaboration self-assessment.

  5. AI Tools Expo

    Teams present to their clients, revise, and give final presentations.
