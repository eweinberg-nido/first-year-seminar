High School · Grade 9 · Course Guide

First Year Seminar

A year-long course that fills both a health and a technology graduation credit. Ninth graders examine their own lives (habits, mental health, choices about substances, identity and relationships) and use a design and technology lens to build solutions for themselves and their community.

Course question How do we design our own solutions, tools and systems to help us maximize our healthy outcomes?
4 units 9 proficiency scales Health + Technology credit

Goals

What the course is for

Ideas that guide every unit:

Informed decisions

Students get the information they need to make the best decisions for their own bodies and well-being.

Safety

Students know how to keep themselves and others safe, mentally and physically.

Respect & support

Students become supportive peers who respect the decisions of others.

Advocacy

Students act as changemakers against injustice and inequity, with particular care for women, children, people of color and the LGBTQ+ community.

Progression

From me to we

Each unit, and the year as a whole, moves from understanding yourself to designing for others.

  1. Self-knowledgeReflection on what works for me
  2. Personal routinesHabit tracking and data
  3. Organization & goalsDigital systems, SMART goals
  4. Individual designTools for myself
  5. Collaborative designTools and messages for our community

Across the course

Shared practices

Threads that recur in every unit.

Design process

Empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test, reflect. Students treat their own systems and campaigns as prototypes.

Process journal

Each student’s notebook is the evidence trail: reflections, research, sketches and iterations.

AI with ownership

“Our words, our ideas, our design.” AI builds, renders or role-plays; students make the decisions.

Information literacy

The CRAAP test (currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose) is applied to every health topic.

Safe-space norms

One mic, step up/step back, “I” statements, the right to pass, S.W.I.M., and anonymous question boxes.

Trusted adults

Every health unit points students to counselors, nurses and trusted adults, with clear escalation when safety is at stake.
