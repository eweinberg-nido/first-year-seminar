Unit 2 · Semester 1 · October – December

Mental Health & the Teen Brain

Students learn how the brain works and how stress affects it. They try out stress-management and executive-function strategies, and build literacy around anxiety, depression and suicide prevention. The unit ends with a team design challenge: using Youth Truth survey data and their own research, teams design a wellness space for teenagers at Nido.

About 20 class days Product: Wellness Space Design Challenge

Driving questions

  • When can we make our brain do what WE want it to?
  • How can we design a “wellness space” for the teen brain?

Students will be able to…

  • Apply understanding of how neurological, situational and environmental factors affect an individual’s mental health.
  • Demonstrate practices that have a positive, long-term impact on their own mental health and executive functioning.
  • Explain how brain structures, neurotransmitters and hormones drive the stress response (fight, flight, freeze) and executive function.
  • Evaluate personal practices (mindfulness, sleep, social media, physical activity) and treat their own systems as prototypes to try, adjust and record.
  • Recognize warning signs and support pathways for anxiety, depression and suicide risk, and know when to involve a trusted adult.
  • Use data and user research to design, prototype and justify a change to a campus space, working as a team.

Approach & key routines

Experiment, don’t prescribe

“You don’t have to like it, you just have to try.” Strategies are offered as experiments, and not every one will work for every student.

Process journal

A running record across the whole unit: reflections, research tables, design iterations and final slides.

Harkness discussion

Student-led discussion with shared norms: share the space, listen actively, disagree respectfully. “A discussion is a team sport!”

Observe → Question → Connect

Why “Empathize” comes first in design: concluding too early projects our own experience onto someone else.

Youth Truth data inquiry

Four data stations (Belonging, Depression & Suicide, Trusted Adult, State of Mind): Notice → Question → Connect → Act.

AI with design ownership

“Our words, our ideas, our design, our sketch. AI creates an image as another iteration.”

Sensitive topics & suicide prevention

Lessons on anxiety and depression open with a scripted disclaimer: “We are not mental health professionals. Please talk to a trusted adult…” The focus is literacy, stigma and help-seeking, not diagnosis. Students are never asked to identify classmates or disclose private experiences.

Trained Nido students deliver the Forefront LEARN Saves Lives training. Parents are emailed beforehand, and teachers coordinate with counselors and plan a step-out option for students.

  • Look for signs
  • Empathize & listen
  • Ask about suicide
  • Reduce the danger
  • Next steps

If a peer asks for secrecy, students learn that safety comes first: they always tell a counselor or trusted adult.

Lesson sequence

Class days as numbered in the Unit 2 slide deck. The deck skips some day numbers.

How the brain works

  1. Technology and us

    Article annotation and a 40-minute Harkness discussion.

  2. Can I trust my brain?

    Perception illusions (Yanny/Laurel, the McGurk effect) and emotional intelligence.

  3. Unit launch

    The driving question and deliverables, a systems audit, and process journal setup.

  4. Brain anatomy

    Label ten structures and map everyday actions to brain regions.

  5. Executive function & technology

    Working memory, flexible thinking and self-control. A before-and-after change to Drive, Calendar or Gmail.

Stress & coping

  1. Quit stressing me out!

    Fight, flight or freeze, plus neurotransmitters and hormones. Students rank the workshop stations.

  2. Brain quiz & workshop stations

    Clear Screens Clear Mind · Stretch & Rest · Walk & Talk · Task Breakdown.

  3. Stress & the teenage brain

    The physiological sigh. Students begin the Personal Stress Management Plan.

  4. Me and my unique brain

    Adolescent development episode with a notetaker and private reflection.

Mental health literacy

  1. Depression

    Stigma warm-up, disclaimer, the Growing Up “Alex” episode, and support resources.

  2. Forefront LEARN training

    A peer-led suicide-prevention session.

  3. LEARN recap & anxiety

    Recall the LEARN steps and the secrecy scenario. Anxiety: contributing factors, coping and support.

  4. What is happening in our world?

    End-of-semester study planner emailed to a guardian. School-design videos, including Nido’s campus master plan.

Wellness Space Design Challenge

  1. Youth Truth data

    How does campus encourage or discourage well-being? Students rotate through the four data stations.

  2. Design research

    Expert groups on Nature, Social, Well-being and Executive Function, each led by a teacher.

  3. Ideating

    Focus groups name their “one must-have”. Sketching with “I like / I wonder” feedback.

  4. Needs & justification

    “___ need a way to ___ because ___”. Feature justifications, AI image iterations, and prototype options.

  5. Final design

    Master deck and roles, two presentation rounds with feedback, and two slides per student.

  6. Presentation prep

    Full rehearsal to a partner group, then a pre-mortem.

  7. Presentation day & submission

    Teams present. Exit ticket: what could Nido actually implement? Students then complete the journal check.
