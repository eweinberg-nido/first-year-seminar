Overview / Unit 3

Unit 3 · Semester 2 · February – April

Harm Reduction & Substances

“Safety First” replaces scare tactics and “Just Say No” with honest, evidence-based, non-judgmental information, and treats abstinence as the safest choice. Students learn how drugs affect the teenage brain, study each major drug class, and practise emergency response. The unit ends with a team harm-reduction campaign in Canva, the course’s first Creative Communicator project.

About 18 class days · Feb 17 – Apr 15 Product: Harm-reduction PSA campaign

Framing questions

The unit has no single driving question. These questions recur across lessons.

  • What is a drug, and how do values and culture shape what we believe about drugs?
  • Have scare tactics and “Just Say No” worked? Why or why not?
  • For each substance: what are the dangers, what are the signs of an emergency, and how should we respond?
  • What message is missing for teens?

Students will be able to…

  • Define a drug as “any substance that, in small amounts, produces changes in the brain, body, or both”, and analyze how values and culture shape beliefs about drug use.
  • Define harm reduction, distinguish it from traditional prevention, and apply the harm-reduction toolbox.
  • Categorize drug-related harms as Physical, Academic, Social-emotional, or Legal, and recognize systemic disparities in legal consequences.
  • Place use on the Continuum of Substance Use without stigmatizing people who use drugs.
  • Compare perceptions of teen drug use with data from Monitoring the Future, NIDA and Chile’s SENDA (2023).
  • Explain the roles of key neurotransmitters (dopamine, acetylcholine, GABA, serotonin) and why the adolescent brain is a “window of vulnerability.”
  • For each drug class, describe effects, harms, and appropriate harm-reduction strategies.
  • Recognize and respond to emergencies: C.U.P.S. for alcohol poisoning, the recovery position, calling 131, and Good Samaritan laws.
  • Evaluate sources with the CRAAP test, analyze school drug policy, and communicate a message responsibly (copyright, fair use, Creative Commons).

Approach

How harm reduction is framed for students, and the norms that make the conversation safe.

We do not…

  • Judge teens who use drugs
  • Encourage or condone drug use
  • Teach how to use drugs

We do…

  • Treat abstinence as a primary strategy
  • Acknowledge that some teens will try drugs
  • Empower students with accurate information
  • Give concrete steps to reduce harm

The harm-reduction toolbox

The strategies are framed for three situations: students who use, students whose friends use, and abstinent students who may face a choice later.

  • Abstinence
  • Develop drug knowledge
  • Dose & dosage
  • Start low, go slow
  • Set & setting
  • Check the substance
  • Know how to respond to an emergency
  • Know drug policy
  • Consider moderation
  • Don’t mix substances
  • Sober sitter (on one wiki page only)

Classroom norms

One Mic

One speaker at a time, so every voice is heard.

Step Up / Step Back

Students who speak often make room; quieter students step in.

Good intent, real impact

Assume good intent, acknowledge impact, and use “I” statements.

Vegas Rule

What is shared in the room stays in the room.

S.W.I.M.

“Someone Who Isn’t Me”: talk about experiences in the third person.

Ideas, not people

Disagree with ideas, not with people. Revisit the norms when opinions clash.

Routines: a 3-minute Mindful Moment and a Do Now open each class. Other routines include table talk and think-pair-share, guided video worksheets, anonymous exit tickets answered in the next class, and movement activities: Philosophical Chairs, sticker gallery walks, and the standard-drink pouring demo.

Lesson sequence

The 2026 sequence as taught. Dates are shown where the source gives them.

Foundations · What is a drug?

  1. Reconnections & “What is a drug?” Tue Feb 17

    A self-care reflection walk, a sticky-note Do Now, the anonymous pre-survey, and the Safety First philosophy.

  2. Norms, “Is it a drug?” & Philosophical Chairs

    Apply the definition (“They are all drugs!”) and debate ten statements. Exit ticket: what should 9th graders know?

  3. Introduction to harm reduction Feb 19

    Build the definition from everyday examples such as seatbelts, then compare perceived drug prevalence with real data.

  4. Motivations & drug-related harms

    Why teens use, and sorting harms into Physical, Academic, Social-emotional and Legal categories. Discussion of systemic legal disparities.

  5. The Continuum of Substance Use

    Map case studies onto the continuum and discuss stigma.

  6. Creative Communicator launch

    The PSA project is announced. Canva onboarding with an Instagram template (L2). No AI design tools.

  7. Drugs & the teenage brain Mon Feb 24

    Neurons and neurotransmitters. Age of first use matters: 1 in 7 who start drinking at 14 develop problems, compared with 1 in 50 who start at 21.

  8. Harm reduction in action: the toolbox

    Scenario practice, then each strategy in turn, with Chilean emergency numbers and drug checking.

  9. Safety at a party

    The Indira and Nick scenarios: environmental risks, and why you should never use alone.

Stimulants, nicotine & design basics

  1. Stimulants

    The Fatima scenario: what “uppers” are, and their effects and harms.

  2. Caffeine madness

    Adenosine, half-life, a caffeine-content bracket game, and the 150 mg daily limit.

  3. Communicators: color & fonts

    HEX palette swap and a tone challenge: one message designed as sad, silly, and professional.

  4. Communicators: layout & design

    Size, white space, flow, and alignment. Students redesign earlier work and reflect on the changes.

  5. Stimulants mini-research

    Cocaine, prescription vs. street amphetamines, and methylphenidate, linked to the “Know drug policy” strategy.

  6. Nicotine & vaping

    Nicotine and the brain, e-cigarette anatomy, the “water vapor” myth, and an audit of students’ own social-media ads.

  7. Vaping research with CRAAP

    Four sources (two for, two against) evaluated with CRAAP, then a synthesis paragraph.

  8. Marketing, misinformation & visual communication

    The agendas behind health information. Students make the L3 A4 nicotine harm-reduction flyer.

Cannabis, alcohol, opioids & psychedelics

  1. Cannabis: fact or fiction

    Sticker gallery walk on seven statements. The teacher models CRAAP on a news article and a Reddit thread, then groups research one statement.

  2. Cannabis, part 2

    THC and CBD, the endocannabinoid system, edibles, Chilean law, and SENDA data. Students re-vote on the opening statements.

  3. Alcohol & depressants

    GABA, a myth quiz, the standard-drink pouring activity, and preventing drink spiking.

  4. Alcohol poisoning response

    C.U.P.S., Good Samaritan laws, recovery-position practice, and a script for calling 131.

  5. Binge drinking

    An empathy activity, “What would you really say?”, and the Teen Talk homework.

  6. Partner emergency scenario Mar 30

    C.U.P.S. review and practising the response steps (L4).

  7. Opioids

    History, mechanism, tolerance, the overdose crisis, and a drug-checking demonstration with pitchers.

  8. Opioid overdose response

    Mixing risks, overdose signs, naloxone, and a news-analysis homework.

  9. Strategy application & intellectual property

    Match strategies to substances, then copyright, fair use and Creative Commons through a scenario.

  10. Psychedelics

    LSD, psilocybin, MDMA and Tusi. Current research, set and setting, and sober sitters.

Assessment, policy & campaign

  1. Emergency scenarios assessment

    Partner role-play: identify the drug, assess symptoms, respond, and brief EMS (played by the teacher).

  2. Zero tolerance & school policy

    The Nido handbook (“major” vs. “intolerable” offenses), counseling, and a critique of punitive messaging.

  3. Campaign launch

    Students study past campaigns. Teams of three choose one strategy and plan three consistent pieces.

  4. Information gathering

    Define the audience and strategy, evaluate sources with CRAAP, and give peer feedback.

  5. A Campaign for a Healthy Future Thu Apr 9

    “What is the harm you are trying to reduce?” Creative Commons imagery and the Collaboration 3.0 target.

  6. Peer feedback & submission Mon Apr 13

    Three-part peer review. The final campaign is due April 15.

Content topics

Harm reduction philosophy

Drug-related harms

Physical / Academic / Social / Legal, and systemic disparities.

Prevalence & perception

Monitoring the Future, SENDA 2023, and the gap between perception and reality.

Social perceptions & media

Neurobiology

Neurotransmitters, myelination, plasticity, and why age of onset matters.

Stimulants

Nicotine & vaping

ENDS, e-liquids, pouches, aerosols, and marketing to teens.

Cannabis

Alcohol & depressants

GABA, standard drinks, binge drinking, drink spiking, consent.

C.U.P.S., Good Samaritan laws, recovery position, dispatcher script.

Opioids

Fentanyl, the overdose crisis, overdose signs, naloxone.

Psychedelics

LSD, psilocybin, MDMA; therapeutic research; risks and emergency signs.

Designer drugs

Tusi as a case study in unpredictable mixtures.

Drug policy & support

Zero tolerance, offense types, counseling, punitive vs. harm-reduction messaging.

Evaluating sources

The CRAAP test, stakeholder agendas, anecdote vs. data.

Creative communication

Intellectual property

Copyright, fair use, Creative Commons.
